Limits and glossary

A short list of what this observatory does not do, followed by the terms needed to read it.

What it does not say

  • It does not predict the result. It is an average of published polls, not a forecast or a seat projection.
  • It does not estimate voting intention. It aggregates what polling houses publish.
  • The range is not a confidence interval. It is the observed min–max among the polls included.
  • It does not correct for demographics. It does not model age, sex, income or territory.
  • The hemicycle is not the Congress. It uses a national district and excludes regional parties.
  • It does not attribute coordination or campaigns. It measures published polls, not actors’ intentions.
  • It does not publish polls during the blackout. From 24 to 28 November 2026 the data and OG files are withdrawn and the endpoints return 410.

Key terms

  • Poll average: weighted mean of published polls in a window. Not a single survey.
  • Range: minimum and maximum observed. Sensitive to a single outlier.
  • IQR: interquartile range; the middle 50% of the polls. More robust than min–max.
  • House effect: the difference between a pollster’s latest result and the average. Not a bias score.
  • Source vs house: a source is where the poll was captured; a house is the pollster. One source can compile several houses; they are not added.
  • D’Hondt: the seat allocation method used in Spain. The hemicycle applies it nationally as a mechanical exercise.
  • Closed and blocked list: voters choose a party list, not the order of its members (Congress).
  • Blackout: the legal period when polls cannot be published, disseminated or reproduced (24–28 November 2026).
